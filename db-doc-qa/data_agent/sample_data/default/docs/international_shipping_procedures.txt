International Shipping Procedures

Document ISP-2025, effective 1 July 2025. Owner: International Trade Desk.

1 Scope
These procedures apply to all shipments that cross a national border. They cover customs documents, customs holds and delays on international routes.

2 Required Documents

2.1 Documents for every international shipment
(a) a commercial invoice showing the value and description of the goods;
(b) a packing list;
(c) the exporter's and importer's tax or customs identification numbers.

2.2 Additional documents
(a) Goods of animal or plant origin need a health certificate.
(b) Goods subject to export control need an export licence number on the invoice.

3 Customs Holds

3.1 What a customs hold means
A shipment in Customs Hold status has been stopped by the customs authority for inspection, payment of duties or missing documents.

3.2 Handling a customs hold
(a) On the first day of the hold, check that every document in Section 2 was submitted with the shipment.
(b) If a document is missing, request it from the sender or the customer within 1 business day.
(c) If duties are due, tell the customer the amount and how to pay.
(d) Record the customs reference number in the tracking record.

3.3 Releasing the shipment
Once customs releases the shipment, the carrier resumes delivery. The expected delivery date is moved by the number of days the shipment was held.

4 International Delays

4.1 Monitoring
Track international shipments daily. Delays of up to 3 days are common on international routes and are monitored without escalation.

4.2 Escalation threshold
An international shipment, including one in Customs Hold, is escalated when it is delayed by 7 days or more. The trade desk opens the escalation case and includes the customs reference number.

4.3 Contact for international escalations
International escalations are sent to the trade desk at trade-desk@example.com.
